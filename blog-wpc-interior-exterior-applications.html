What if one material could transform your walls, floors and outdoor spaces?

Green Plastwood · SNG Eco India Pvt. Ltd.Updated October 20267 min read

When selecting materials for a building or interior project, designers often have to work with multiple products.

One material for walls.

Another for flooring.

Another for exterior elevations.

Another for pergolas.

And another for decorative architectural elements.

But what if a single material category could offer solutions across many of these applications?

That is where WPC — Wood Plastic Composite — becomes interesting.

The Green Plastwood range from SNG Eco India includes products designed for a wide variety of interior and exterior applications, ranging from doors and boards to decking, wall cladding, louvers, planks, pergolas and gazebos.

What is WPC?

WPC stands for Wood Plastic Composite.

It is a composite material category used across various architectural and building applications.

Green Plastwood positions its WPC range as an alternative material for applications where characteristics such as moisture resistance, termite resistance, weather resistance, fire retardancy and recyclability are important considerations.

The important point is that WPC is not one single product.

Different profiles and formats are developed for different applications.

1. WPC deck flooring

Outdoor flooring has to deal with a different set of requirements than conventional indoor flooring.

Green Plastwood offers WPC deck flooring as a low-maintenance decking solution.

The catalogue specifies:

  • Dimensions: 150 mm × 23 mm
  • Standard lengths: 8 ft and 10 ft
  • Surface finishes: Brushing and sanding
  • Colours: Teak, Brown and Grey

The catalogue shows applications including:

Where can WPC decking work?

Instead of leaving an elevation completely plain, designers can introduce texture and material variation through wall cladding.

Green Plastwood WPC wall cladding is designed for this type of application.

145 mm × 20.5 mm

with standard lengths of:

8 ft and 10 ft

The listed finishes are:

How do you choose the right WPC product?

The biggest mistake in material selection is starting with the product rather than the application.

Instead, follow this process.

Step 1: identify the application

Is it:

  • Wall cladding?
  • Flooring?
  • Ceiling?
  • Partition?
  • Louver?
  • Pergola?
  • Door?
  • Furniture?
  • Exterior elevation?

Step 2: determine the environment

Is the product going indoors or outdoors?

Will it experience moisture?

Will it be exposed to weather?

Step 3: select the profile

Different applications require different dimensions and profiles.

Step 4: choose the finish

Green Plastwood's catalogue includes finishes such as:

  • Brushing
  • Sanding

depending on the product.

Step 5: select the colour

Common colours across several exterior products include:

  • Teak
  • Brown
  • Grey

Step 6: plan installation

Check:

  • Substructure
  • Fixing system
  • Screws
  • Brackets
  • Clips
  • Other accessories

Step 7: confirm quantity

Calculate the required surface area and account for the installation system and project-specific requirements.

WPC interior & exterior applications: the bigger picture

The real advantage of looking at a complete WPC product ecosystem is that the designer doesn't have to think about each product in isolation.

For example, a single project could potentially combine:

  • WPC wall cladding
  • WPC louvers
  • WPC decking
  • WPC pergola
  • WPC doors

The catalogue demonstrates how Green Plastwood's product portfolio spans both interior and exterior applications.

This opens up opportunities to maintain a more consistent material language across different parts of a project.

Final thoughts

WPC is no longer something that needs to be considered only for one specific application.

From interior doors and furniture to exterior cladding, decking, louvers, pergolas and gazebos, the Green Plastwood catalogue presents a broad range of possible applications.

The important question isn't:

"Is WPC good?"

The better question is:

"Which WPC product is appropriate for my specific application?"

That decision should be based on the product specification, project environment, design requirements, installation system and expected use.
